02 · LEARN THE RULES

How do lenses form images?

A lens refracts light. The image appears where refracted rays meet, or where their backward extensions appear to meet.

🔵 Convex lens

A convex lens is thicker at the centre than at the edges. In air, it is a converging lens: parallel rays converge towards the principal focus.

  • For an object beyond F, it generally forms a real, inverted image.
  • Between F and the optical centre, it forms a virtual, erect, magnified image.
  • At F, the emerging rays are parallel and the image is at infinity in the ideal thin-lens model.
f > 0

🟠 Concave lens

A concave lens is thinner at the centre than at the edges. In air, it is a diverging lens: parallel rays spread out as if they came from its principal focus.

  • For a real object, it forms a virtual image.
  • The image is erect and diminished.
  • The image lies between the optical centre and the focus on the object's side.
f < 0
03 · THE EQUATIONS

Three formulas worth remembering

Thin lens formula

1/f = 1/v − 1/u

This is the formula using the Cartesian sign convention, where the object distance u for a real object is negative.

In this simulator, u is entered as a positive distance magnitude, so the equivalent working formula is:

1/f = 1/v + 1/u

Magnification

m = hᵢ/hₒ = v/u

With signed Cartesian distances, magnification is v/u. In the simulator's positive object-distance format, it is calculated as m = −v/u.

  • m > 0: erect image
  • m < 0: inverted image
  • |m| > 1: enlarged; |m| < 1: diminished

Power of a lens

P = 1/f (in metres)

Power is measured in dioptres (D). A converging convex lens has positive power; a diverging concave lens has negative power.

Example: A convex lens with f = +0.20 m has power P = 1/0.20 = +5 D.
