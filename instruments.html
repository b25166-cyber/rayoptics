📘 NCERT concept cards

1. Simple microscope

A simple microscope is a convex lens of short focal length. The object is placed within its focal length, producing a virtual, erect, magnified image. Its magnifying power is approximately D/f for relaxed viewing and 1 + D/f when the final image is at the near point.

2. Compound microscope

A compound microscope uses two convex lenses. The objective has a very short focal length and creates a real, inverted, enlarged intermediate image. The eyepiece acts as a magnifier to produce the final virtual image.

3. Astronomical telescope

The objective has a large focal length and collects light from distant objects. The eyepiece has a shorter focal length and magnifies the intermediate image. In normal adjustment, lens separation is approximately fₒ + fₑ.

4. Magnifying power

Magnifying power is the ratio of the angle subtended by the final image at the eye to the angle subtended by the object when viewed directly. It is an angular magnification, not simply the physical size of the image.

5. Near point and relaxed eye

The conventional near point for a normal eye is D = 25 cm. Viewing at the near point can increase magnifying power, while a final image at infinity allows relaxed viewing.

6. Compare the instruments

A simple microscope uses one convex lens. A compound microscope uses an objective and an eyepiece to view tiny nearby objects. An astronomical telescope uses an objective and an eyepiece to view distant objects.
