02 / Understand the basics

Start with the ideas needed to understand spherical mirrors.

What is reflection?

Reflection is the return of light into the same medium after it strikes a surface.

  • The angle of incidence equals the angle of reflection.
  • The incident ray, reflected ray, and normal lie in the same plane.

What is a spherical mirror?

A spherical mirror has a reflecting surface that forms part of a sphere.

  • Concave: reflecting surface curves inward.
  • Convex: reflecting surface bulges outward.

03 / Important mirror terms

Learn to identify these points in a ray diagram.

Pole (P)

The geometric centre of the reflecting surface of the mirror.

Centre of curvature (C)

The centre of the sphere of which the mirror is a part.

Radius of curvature (R)

The distance between the pole and the centre of curvature.

Principal axis

The straight line passing through the pole and centre of curvature.

Principal focus (F)

For a spherical mirror under the paraxial approximation, rays parallel to the principal axis converge at, or appear to diverge from, the principal focus after reflection.

Focal length (f)

The distance between the pole and the principal focus.

04 / Mirror formula and magnification

Use the Cartesian sign convention consistently when substituting values.

Mirror formula

1/f = 1/v + 1/u

u = object distance, v = image distance, and f = focal length.

R = 2f

For a spherical mirror in the paraxial approximation, the radius of curvature is twice the focal length.

Linear magnification

m = hᵢ/hₒ = −v/u

hᵢ is image height and hₒ is object height.

  • m is negative: image is inverted.
  • m is positive: image is erect.
  • |m| greater than 1: enlarged image.
  • |m| less than 1: diminished image.

07 / NCERT-style solved example

Apply the mirror formula step by step.

Question

An object is placed 30 cm in front of a concave mirror of focal length 15 cm. Find the image distance and magnification.

Step 1 — Sign convention: u = −30 cm and f = −15 cm.

Step 2 — Mirror formula: 1/f = 1/v + 1/u

1/v = 1/f − 1/u = −1/15 + 1/30 = −1/30

Therefore, v = −30 cm.

Step 3 — Magnification: m = −v/u = −(−30)/(−30) = −1.

The image is real, inverted, and the same size as the object.
