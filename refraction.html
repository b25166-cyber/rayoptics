02 / What is refraction?

Understand the physical idea before using the formula.

Definition

Refraction is the change in direction of light when it passes obliquely from one transparent medium to another because its speed changes.

  • The frequency of light remains unchanged at the boundary.
  • Its speed and wavelength change when it enters a medium with a different refractive index.
  • At normal incidence, the ray does not bend, although its speed changes.

Why does light bend?

Light travels at different speeds in different media. When it enters the second medium obliquely, one part of the wavefront changes speed before another part. This changes the direction of propagation.

Entering a medium with a higher refractive index generally bends the ray towards the normal. Entering a lower-index medium bends it away from the normal.

03 / Snell's law

The relationship between the angles and the refractive indices of two media.

n₁ sin i = n₂ sin r

n₁ and n₂

Refractive indices of the first and second media.

i

Angle of incidence, measured between the incident ray and normal.

r

Angle of refraction, measured between the refracted ray and normal.

For a given pair of media and a fixed wavelength, the ratio sin i / sin r is constant. This is the basis of Snell's law.

04 / Refractive index

Refractive index tells us how much the speed of light changes in a medium.

Absolute refractive index

n = c/v

c is the speed of light in vacuum and v is its speed in the medium.

For ordinary transparent media, n is generally greater than or equal to 1.

Relative refractive index

n₂₁ = n₂/n₁ = v₁/v₂

This compares the refractive indices of two media. It helps predict how a ray bends as it crosses their boundary.

A larger refractive index means light travels more slowly in that medium.

06 / Refraction through a glass slab

For a plane-parallel slab surrounded by the same medium, the emergent ray is parallel to the incident ray.

At the first surface, the ray refracts into the glass. At the second parallel surface, it refracts out again. The two direction changes leave the emergent ray parallel to the incident ray, but shifted sideways. This shift is called lateral displacement.

d = t sin(i − r) / cos r

Here, d is lateral displacement, t is slab thickness, i is the angle of incidence, and r is the angle inside the slab.

The formula applies to a plane-parallel slab with the same external medium on both sides.

07 / NCERT-style solved example

Calculate the refracted angle using Snell's law.

Question

A ray of light travels from air (n₁ = 1.00) into glass (n₂ = 1.50). The angle of incidence is 30°. Find the angle of refraction.

Step 1: Apply Snell's law: n₁ sin i = n₂ sin r.

Step 2: Substitute values: 1.00 × sin 30° = 1.50 × sin r.

Therefore, sin r = 0.5 / 1.5 = 1/3.

r = sin⁻¹(1/3) ≈ 19.5°.

The refracted ray bends towards the normal because it enters a medium with a higher refractive index.
